HeresThe.AnimalHoneybee: the flower map you dance with your whole body
  • A forager bee who finds a good patch of flowers flies home and dances it out for her hive mates, tracing a tight figure eight on the honeycomb.
  • The angle of the dance's waggle run compared to straight up tells the other bees the direction to fly relative to the sun.
  • How long she waggles tells them how far to go, so bees can fly straight to a flower patch they have never seen before.
  • Karl von Frisch spent decades decoding this dance language, and in 1973 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Konrad Lorenz and Nikolaas Tinbergen for their work on animal behavior.
  • The dance happens in the pitch-dark hive, so the other bees read it entirely by touch and sound, not sight.

These days, most people consume the news electronically. When I became an adult, I had no desire to subscribe to a news outlet — choosing one felt like choosing an entire worldview. Rather than be swayed by polarized writing and clickbait tactics, I decided it was better to be uninformed than misinformed.

In July 2026, while working on a project, I had the idea for this website: an automated news report that pulled from dozens of sources and hundreds of articles daily. And the best part? It could do all of that while leaving the opinions behind.

Ten days later, HeresThe.News was ready. I hadn't planned on releasing it publicly, but I soon realized that many people like me were dissatisfied with the current media options.

My promise with this project is the same as it was in the beginning.

No ads. No bait. Just news.

How it works

  1. 1

    Read everything

    Each morning, AI agents fetch the latest headlines from all 46 sources below — outlets chosen to disagree with each other, spanning left, right, wire services, and primary sources.

  2. 2

    Find the facts

    Coverage of the same event is clustered and compared. What survives is what outlets actually agree happened — framing, adjectives, and speculation are left behind. Cross-checking follows each story wherever it leads — sometimes beyond the roster below — and every outlet used is named on the story.

  3. 3

    Label every claim

    verified — multiple independent outlets or a primary source confirm it.
    reported — it traces to a single source so far.
    disputed — outlets disagree, and we show you the disagreement instead of picking a side.
    developing — fast-moving; numbers may change.

    Syndicated wire copy counts as ONE origin, no matter how many outlets reprint it.

    Every fact names its sources, and every story links to the full coverage.

  4. 4

    Check our own work

    Every day, five claims from the previous edition are randomly re-verified against their cited sources by a separate audit. The running score is published in the footer of every edition — including the misses.

  5. 5

    Humans set rules, not stories

    The editorial rules — what counts as verified, how headlines must be written, what gets excluded — are fixed and written by a person. The AI applies them; no one picks or spins individual stories, and no advertiser can buy influence because there are no ads.

    Got something wrong? editor@heresthe.news — corrected promptly, noted publicly.
